E‑Ticketing & Gate Management — User Manual

This manual explains how to run the ticketing system for Pravasi Sangama 2026. It is written for the administrator, but it also covers what the field agents and gate staff see, so you can answer their questions and train them without needing a second document.

No technical knowledge is assumed. Anything you need to type is shown in bold.

1. What the system does

The system replaces paper tickets with digital passes carrying QR codes.

  1. A field agent registers a guest and issues a ticket.
  2. The guest receives the pass by WhatsApp, email, or on paper.
  3. At the venue, gate staff scan the QR code to admit them.
  4. You watch it all happen live from the admin dashboard.

Every ticket is traceable to the agent who issued it and the unit they were posted at. Nothing is anonymous.

2. Who uses it

(scanner)		 Scan QR codes and admit guests. Issue tickets, see guest lists, see any totals.
Why agents cannot cancel tickets. Only you can. This is deliberate — it means a mistake at a registration desk cannot quietly erase a record, and every cancellation has one accountable person behind it.

3. For the Administrator

3.1 Signing in

Go to the web address for the system and enter your username (or your email address) and password. You will land on the System Overview.

Your session lasts 2 hours, then asks you to sign in again. This is shorter than an agent's session on purpose: your account can do far more damage if a laptop is left open.

3.2 The five sections

Every screen has the same navigation bar across the top. You are always one click from any section.

SectionWhat it is for
System OverviewLive totals and a running feed of gate scans as they happen. Your event-day screen.
Agent ApprovalsNew agents who have registered and are waiting for you to let them in.
Agent DirectoryEvery approved agent, with how many tickets each has issued.
Ticket LedgerEvery ticket in the system, searchable and filterable.
Gate ManagementCreate gates and set or change their PINs.

3.3 Approving an agent

An agent who registers cannot sign in until you approve them. Open Agent Approvals and press Approve or Reject.

  • Approve — they can sign in immediately and start issuing.
  • Reject — final. They would have to register again from scratch.
This is your main security control. Anyone can fill in the registration form. Nobody can issue a single ticket until you press Approve. Check the name and mobile number against your own list of volunteers before approving.

3.4 Finding a ticket

Open Ticket Ledger. You can filter by division, unit and agent, and search by guest name, mobile number, or ticket number. The summary cards at the top update to match whatever you have filtered.

The cards count every matching ticket, even when the table below shows only the most recent few hundred. If the table says it is showing a portion, the totals are still complete.

3.5 If an agent leaves or a phone is lost

Open Agent Directory, find the agent, and deactivate them. They are signed out and cannot issue anything further. Their existing tickets stay valid and stay on the record — deactivating a person never invalidates guests who already hold passes.

4. For Agents (field staff)

4.1 Signing in

Agents go to the same web address and enter their mobile number (or email) and password. That is the whole login — there is no unit code or location PIN to remember.

The system already knows which unit each agent belongs to, and writes every ticket against that unit automatically. An agent cannot issue against a unit they are not posted to, because they are never asked which unit they are at.

An agent session lasts 8 hours — a full shift.

4.2 Issuing a ticket

  1. Press New Registration.
  2. Fill in the guest's name, mobile number, and email if they have one.
  3. Choose the ticket type.
  4. Enter how many children below 12 are coming.
  5. Press submit. The pass appears immediately.

4.3 Ticket types

TypeAdults admittedQR panels on the printed pass
Normal12 — one location, one entry code
VIP45 — one location, four entry codes
VVIP45 — one location, four entry codes
SVIP45 — one location, four entry codes

VIP, VVIP and SVIP admit exactly the same number of people. They differ only in pricing and access zone, never in headcount.

Children below 12 are free and are not counted. They do not use up an entry code and do not reduce the adult count. They are recorded only so catering and crowd safety know the real number of bodies in the venue.

4.4 The QR codes on a pass

  • Entry codes (“Guest 1 QR”, and so on) — each admits one person, once.
  • Location QR — opens a map to the venue. It is not a ticket and will not admit anyone. Guests can scan it as often as they like.

4.5 Sending the pass to the guest

After issuing, the agent can:

  • WhatsApp — sends the pass as an image. The usual choice.
  • Email — if the guest gave an address.
  • Print / Save as PDF — opens the printer dialogue; choosing “Save as PDF” produces a file named after the ticket.
  • Save as Image — downloads the pass to the phone.

4.6 My Registrations

Agents see a list of every ticket they have personally issued, with guest names, ticket types, adults, children and time. They cannot see anyone else's.

5. For Gate Staff

5.1 Signing in at a gate

Gate staff open the scanner page and enter the gate code and a PIN of 4 to 6 digits. A gate is a place, not a person — volunteers on the same gate share the PIN, and scans are recorded against the gate.

5.2 Scanning

Point the camera at a QR code. The whole screen changes colour:

ColourMeaningWhat to do
GREEN Admitted Let them in.
AMBER Already used This code has been scanned before. Do not admit. Call a supervisor.
RED Not valid Cancelled, unknown, or not a ticket. Do not admit.

The colours are the whole instruction. Staff do not need to read the small text to know what to do, which matters in a queue and in poor light.

5.3 When the internet drops

The scanner keeps working. Scans are stored on the phone and sent up as soon as the signal returns.

While offline, an admitted guest shows PENDING SYNC in amber rather than solid green. Let them in anyway. A gate that stops moving is a worse problem than a scan confirmed a few minutes late.

Honest limitation. If two phones are offline at the same time, the same QR code could be accepted at both. When they reconnect, the second one is recorded as a duplicate and raised as an alert on your dashboard — but that person is already inside. Watch for those alerts on event day.

6. Security — in plain terms

ProtectionWhat it means for you
Nobody joins by themselves Registering is not joining. An administrator must approve every agent before they can issue anything.
Passwords are never stored Only a scrambled, one-way version is kept. Nobody — including us — can read a user's password out of the database.
QR codes are not stored either The system keeps only a fingerprint of each code. Even with full database access, nobody can reconstruct a working ticket.
Ticket numbers cannot be guessed They are random, not sequential. Holding one ticket tells you nothing about any other, and nobody can work out how many have been sold.
A code admits once Re-scanning the same code always reports a duplicate. Two staff scanning the same pass at the same instant cannot both admit.
Every attempt is recorded Admissions, duplicates and rejections are all logged with the gate and time. A burst of duplicates at one gate is the signature of a copied ticket.
Sessions expire Administrator 2 hours, agent 8 hours. A phone left on a table does not stay signed in overnight.
Guessing is throttled Repeated failed sign-in attempts are slowed automatically.
Instant revocation Deactivating an agent signs them out immediately, without affecting guests who already hold valid passes.

6.1 What you are responsible for

The system cannot protect against these. You must:

  • Approve carefully. Approval is the gate. Check each name against your volunteer list.
  • Change the starting passwords and gate PINs before the event. The installation defaults are known and must not survive into the live event.
  • Rotate a gate PIN if a volunteer leaves mid-event.
  • Deactivate promptly when a phone is lost or someone stops working.
  • Never share an administrator account. One person, one login — otherwise the audit trail cannot tell you who did what.

8. Common questions

A guest lost their ticket.

Find them in the Ticket Ledger by name or mobile number to confirm they are genuine, then have their agent issue a replacement. The original codes should be cancelled so the lost pass cannot also be used.

An agent says they cannot sign in.

Check Agent Approvals — they are probably still waiting for approval. If they were approved, check they have not been deactivated in Agent Directory.

The dashboard shows no data.

It will say so plainly rather than showing numbers. If it is blank or reports a connection problem, the app cannot reach the server — that is a technical issue, not a data loss. Nothing already recorded is affected.

A guest arrives with a screenshot of someone else's pass.

The scanner shows amber for the second person. The code has already been used and will not admit twice, no matter how good the copy is.

Can we still work if the venue Wi-Fi fails?

Yes for scanning — it queues and syncs later. Issuing new tickets does need a connection, so keep a mobile hotspot at each registration desk.
